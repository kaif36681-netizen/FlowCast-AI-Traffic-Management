
  F L O W C A S T
  Predicting traffic 30 minutes ahead on a 25-segment city corridor

==========================================================================
  1. THE PROBLEM
==========================================================================

  A city traffic control room can see the road as it is right now, but by
  the time a jam appears it is too late to prevent it.

  This system answers one question:

      Given everything we know up to now,
      what will each stretch of road look like in 30 minutes?

  Four answers per stretch: how many vehicles, how fast, how long to drive
  it, and how likely an accident is.


==========================================================================
  2. THE DATA (and what was wrong with it)
==========================================================================

  Sensor readings received                  178,468   
  Road segments                                  25   
  Period covered                     1 Jan - 31 May 2025   

  Problems found and fixed:

  Impossible readings quarantined               712   negative counts, 300 km/h
  Duplicate rows removed                      1,759   sensor sent twice
  Missing time slots restored                 5,203   <-- the important one
  Weather spellings unified                 13 -> 4   'RAIN', 'rainy', 'Rain'

  Why the missing slots matter most: if you ask the computer for "the
  previous reading" and an 8am row simply does not exist, it hands you
  7am and calls it 8am. Every calculation after that is quietly wrong.


==========================================================================
  3. THE TRAP IN THE DATA
==========================================================================

  One column, "congestion level", turned out to be nothing more than the
  vehicle count sorted into four buckets. Checked against the rows that
  already had a label, the rule matched 99.64% of the time.

  So it is not independent information. Hand it to the computer and it
  "predicts" congestion almost perfectly - because it was given the answer.

  This system refuses that shortcut. Five automated tests exist purely to
  make sure it stays refused.


==========================================================================
  4. RESULTS - how well does it predict?
==========================================================================

  Predicting how many vehicles pass in the next 30 minutes:

  Lazy guess (assume no change)         16.0% error   free, no computer
  Best model (XGBoost)                   8.9% error   <-- twice as good

  That is a 45% reduction in error against the lazy guess.
  The comparison is the point: 8.9% on its own means nothing.

  Every model tried, worst to best:

    Mean baseline                        83.3%  ##################################################################
    Persistence (naive)                  16.0%  ############
    Linear Regression (from scratch)     13.0%  ##########
    Linear Regression (sklearn)          12.3%  #########
    Decision Tree                        10.4%  ########
    Random Forest                         9.3%  #######
    XGBoost                               8.9%  #######

==========================================================================
  5. THE HONEST PART - what did NOT work
==========================================================================

  Target: congestion class                    0.768   needed 0.80  -- MISSED
  Target: accident risk                       0.643   needed 0.75  -- MISSED

  Why did accident prediction fail? I tested it properly.

  I built a deliberately CHEATING version, allowed to see information no
  real forecast could ever have. The cheat improved things by +0.006.

  Essentially nothing. So the answer is not "try harder" - the information
  needed to predict accidents is simply not in this data. What is missing
  is road layout, lane counts, and where crashes happened before.

  Knowing a problem is unsolvable with the data you have is a real finding.

  The ranking is still useful, even at low accuracy:

  Top-decile lift                             2.64x   patrol the riskiest 10%
      -> you find 2.6 times as many incidents as patrolling at random.

==========================================================================
  6. HOW SURE IS IT? (confidence check)
==========================================================================

  The system gives a range, not just a single number. A range is
  only worth showing if it is actually right, so it was checked:

  Range promised to be right         80% of the time   
  Range actually right               80.7% of the time   <-- honest

  The range also widens when the road is harder to predict:

    Off-peak, dry                    +/- 90 vehicles   
    Peak                             +/- 211 vehicles   
    Peak + rain                      +/- 230 vehicles   
    Rain                             +/- 94 vehicles
